## Themes to sell (agreed)

1. **Per student, not per class.** For someone everywhere else, questions come
   from a shared library and every student in the course sees the same set.
   Here they are written fresh, from a plain description of how this one
   student struggles, at their exact level and in their exact curriculum. Two
   students never receive the same questions.
2. **The gap bridge (framed without saying "the gap").** Practice connects
   what happens in a session to every other day of the week. The student
   leaves the weekly session, and the work they do in between is recognisably
   a continuation of it, not a separate generic chore. The lesson is one
   point on a continuous line, and every other point is practice.
3. **Engaged all week, productively.** A child works every day, in short
   focused bursts, and every burst feeds the next day's work. The parent does
   not chase a spread of worksheets and lost printouts; the child has one
   daily thing and it is always the right thing.
4. **Free is not used.** Many of the platform names a parent already knows are
   free. Free is not the problem. Engagement is. A bank of unlimited free
   practice a child never opens is worth less than ten focused minutes with
   questions built for them. The offer is not "access to content", it is a
   daily habit that actually happens.
5. **Proof, not promises.** A weekly report that says plainly: this is the
   trend, these are the topics, this is what is strong and what needs work. A
   parent sees the child's week in a page, not a log.

## Anti-slogans (write against these)

- Never lead with features ("marking, analytics, automation").
- Never lead with the technology (no model names, no "AI" as the headline).
- Never describe it in competitor terms (no naming rivals, no "like X but").
- No claims we cannot stand behind at launch. Honesty over invented scale.
